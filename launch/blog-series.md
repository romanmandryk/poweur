# Poweur blog series

Five complete editorial drafts by Roman Mandryk: a short opening announcement, an
accompanying motivation essay, and three weekly follow-ups. Dates follow the existing October 13,
2026 launch target. These are proposed publication dates;
nothing has been published or scheduled. Each post keeps `draft: true` and includes a
title, description, author and date for transfer into a publishing system.

## Suggested announcement copy

**October 13:** We keep rebuilding the same three things inside every app: identity,
messaging and shared data. I’m building Poweur to make those foundations open—and to make
working with people and agents less dependent on which company owns the workspace.

**October 20:** A prototype needs room to grow. An MVP needs a shape you can finish.
Customers and plugins open the garden again. Some thoughts on when software developers
should be gardeners, and when they should become architects.

**October 27:** A brief, a price list and a draft in a shared folder: a surprisingly useful
starting point for people and agents working together. The interesting work is making the
formats open, the permissions clear and the tools pleasant to use.

**November 3:** A neighbourhood directory can help you find a plumber without owning your
relationship with the plumber. What would more of the internet look like if discovery
worked that way?

## Editorial grounding

The product ideas come from the original launch draft, `apps/site/index.html`,
`apps/site/architecture.html`, and the roadmap—especially EPIC-010 (agents), EPIC-020
(storage), EPIC-029 (apps) and EPIC-032 (feeds and indexers). Roadmap scenarios are presented
as goals, not shipped features. This writing does not mark any product epic complete.

The motivation essay distinguishes control of keys from control of a hosted domain name,
and explains that Poweur currently uses federated relays. It avoids promises of automatic
name migration, universal browser compatibility or a live demo bot.

Historical and security references are linked beside the relevant passages. Martin's
own 2022 essay supports the gardener metaphor and the difficulty of coordinating a large
story; the post does not speculate about whether he will finish the novels.

The posts stand alone, so each can be published without links to future articles. Their
frontmatter descriptions can also serve as newsletter previews or CMS excerpts.
