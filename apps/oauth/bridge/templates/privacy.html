Privacy and retention

{{.Name}} lets applications sign you in with a Poweur ID. It never holds your keys: you approve each sign-in on your own device, and this service checks the signature.

What it keeps, and for how long

  • A sign-in in progress — the application, the Poweur ID you typed and a coarse browser label (such as “Firefox on macOS”), for {{$v.Txn}}, or up to an hour while you create an ID.
  • Authorization codes — {{$v.Code}}; access tokens — {{$v.AccessToken}}. Only hashes are stored.
  • Your session on this service — {{$v.Session}}, in a first-party cookie. Signing out ends it.
  • What you allowed each application to see — until you revoke it at your account, or {{$v.Consents}} after it was last used.
  • Recent sign-ins (application, time, browser label) — {{$v.SignIns}}, so you can review them at your account.
  • Security log (events such as a reused code or a suspended application; no message content) — {{$v.Audit}}.
  • Applications registered by developers — until the developer or the operator deletes them.

What applications learn

Only what you allow on the consent page. {{if $v.Pairwise}}The account identifier (sub) is different for every application, so applications cannot match you up by it. Your Poweur ID itself is shared only when you allow it.{{else}}The account identifier (sub) is the same for every application.{{end}} Your name and picture come from your public profile and are sent only when you allow them.

What it does not do

  • No advertising, analytics or third-party scripts; pages load nothing from other sites. The one exception: when you choose a name for a new Poweur ID, your browser asks the ID provider whether it is free.
  • No passwords and no private keys, ever.
  • This service does not store IP addresses: it uses them briefly, in memory, to limit request rates. The operator's web server may keep ordinary access logs.
